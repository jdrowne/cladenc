# Issues have no fixed form

There is no house layout for an issue's title or body. An issue is written in whatever form best says what it has to say. The one test of it is whether a session that has never seen the conversation can act on it, given the code and the memories.

Many of the issues here open with `Goal` and a list of what the issue relies on, and close with `Sources`, `Rules for the code` and `Working rules`. That is a pattern they share and not a rule. No section is owed, and no length either.

**Why:** A fixed form pushes content into headings it does not fit and makes an issue fill sections that say nothing particular to it, and the problem ends up under text that every other issue carries as well.

**How to apply:**

- Do not mirror an earlier issue's layout for its own sake. Do not add, rename or reorder sections to match the neighbours, and do not treat a divergence from them as a problem.
- The form is free and the substance is not. What still holds: one indivisible problem per issue, per [[one-indivisible-problem-per-issue]]; the words of [[the-paper-is-a-starting-point]]; the user's point restated and never copied, per [[never-quote-the-user-verbatim]]; and, where an issue is handed to a person, what they have to decide, per [[before-an-issue-is-labelled-needs-decision]].
- No other memory sets a part, heading or list an issue must have; a memory about what an issue says governs its words, never its layout.

An issue need not repeat a rule that holds for all the work, since such a rule has a home of its own. The clean-room rule is [[the-work-is-clean-room]], and the rules for the code are [[the-checks-the-code-faces]] and [[tests-are-written-before-code]].
