# The first harness is the primogenitor

Two words, two things:

- The primogenitor is the harness a directed evolution starts from. It is the one harness that is no child.
- A progenitor is the harness that procreates in a procreation. Any created harness can be one, the primogenitor among them.

Parent is not the word for either. The harness that procreates is the progenitor.

**Why:** It is the user's rule, both the telling apart and the choice of progenitor over parent. With progenitor used for the first harness, a reader cannot tell whether a sentence is about the first harness or about the harness that procreates in the procreation at hand.

**How to apply:** Say primogenitor and progenitor in replies, issues, commits, the README, the help, what `cladenc` prints and the names in the code: the flag `--primogenitor`, the key `progenitor` of the journal's `procreation_started` record, the column `progenitor` of `cladenc report`. Never write progenitor for the first harness as such, and never write parent for the harness that procreates. An older issue that says progenitor may mean the first harness, and one that says parent means the progenitor; correct each when the issue is brought up to date, per [[an-issue-is-brought-up-to-date-before-work]].

Mine, not the user's: `Path::parent` of the standard library keeps its name, since it names a directory and no harness.
