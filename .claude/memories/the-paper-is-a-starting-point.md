# The paper is a starting point

`cladenc` takes its ideas from the HGM paper (arXiv:2510.21614) and is not bound to the paper's vocabulary, notation or details. Where a plainer, more natural name exists than the paper's, use it: in option names, journal keys, code identifiers, the issue plan and the README. `cladenc` may also depart from the paper's method wherever a change makes it better.

**Why:** On 2026-11-14 the user asked why the plan in issue #1 named the agent a run starts from as the paper does, when `progenitor` was available. My reason was fidelity to the paper. The user held that the paper's terms are not needed where a more natural one exists, and that the paper is to be followed in its ideas, not to the letter. They added that `cladenc` is to be intellectual property owned by Jose Drowne (`LICENSE.txt` reserves all rights), so it is free to move away from the paper as it improves. Later the same day the user, who did not know what an expansion was, asked why the search's words were not biological ones when `cladenc` is named after the clade, and pointed to keeping things simple. I renamed the step that makes a new harness from expansion to mutation without putting the word to them first (issue #16), and they turned it down: to mutate is to change oneself, as a Gödel machine does, while a harness here is left as it was and it is its child that is changed. They then proposed procreation, and issue #17 renamed the step to procreation and its program to procreator.

**How to apply:** Name things for what they are; `progenitor`, and `genealogy` for what the paper calls the tree, are the user's own examples. What the paper calls an agent is a `harness` here: the user thinks of `cladenc` as improving harnesses and found the paper's word unclear. Mention the paper's name for an idea once, where the plan or the README maps ideas to the paper, so a reader can still find it there. Do not write point-by-point reconciliations with the paper's text, and do not reject an improvement because the paper does it differently. The clean-room rule is separate and unchanged: the paper is the only source, and the authors' code is never read. My own reading, not something the user said: the rule covers the paper's Greek-letter symbols too, which is why the plan says `--genealogy-growth` and `--confidence`. When a thing needs a name, look first for a simple word from biology or family descent that says what the thing is, in keeping with progenitor, parent, child, genealogy, clade and procreation; a tree-search word or a word of the paper's is not a reason by itself. The word has to be true of what happens: a parent is never changed, so no word that says it changes fits. My own readings, not the user's words: put a new word that people will type or read to the user, with what it would look like, before renaming anything, since the word I chose alone was wrong; and `evaluation` and `evaluator` stay because they are plain and biology offers nothing simpler, which the user has not objected to.
